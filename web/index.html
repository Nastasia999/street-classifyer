About the method

Street Morphotypes: a data-driven street typology from OpenStreetMap

Anastasiia Erokhina · 2026

The question

A street is two things at once: a link in the movement network and a place lined by buildings and activities (Marshall 2005; Jones, Roberts & Morris 2007). Design guidance is increasingly written per street type, yet types are usually assigned by hand from the road hierarchy, which describes only the link. Can both roles be measured from open data alone, and does the resulting typology carry over from one city to another?

Method

  1. Segments. The OpenStreetMap network is split from junction to junction; the two carriageways of a divided road keep their own sides.
  2. Link. Space syntax angular segment analysis: integration (NAIN) and through-movement (NACH) for the whole city (Rn) and within a 10-minute walk (800 m); lanes, public transport routes and stops.
  3. Place. Measured in one-sided strips beyond the carriageway edge: street-wall continuity, organisations, greenery and the floor-area shares of residential, commercial, social and industrial use.
  4. Two clusterings. Each block is clustered on its own (k-means, k chosen by silhouette), so neither swamps the other. The morphotype is the pair: transport class × context class. A Gaussian mixture gives each segment a confidence; uncertain segments are flagged as borderline.

Case study: Irkutsk and Wrocław

Two cities of similar size (620,000 and 670,000 people), each with a historic core and large post-war housing estates, processed with the same defaults: 4,186 and 6,632 classified segments.

  • The transport typology is universal. The four classes found independently in both cities (local access streets, ordinary streets, PT streets with stops, PT arterials) are practically the same: a city labelled by the other city's model agrees with its own labels at ARI 0.91.
  • The context typology is partly city-specific (ARI 0.44–0.61). Built-up and green streets recur, but “commercial” is relative, and Wrocław's institutional streets have no counterpart in Irkutsk. A typology meant to carry design guidance across cities should be trained on a diverse pool of cities, not on one.

Limitations

Everything rests on OpenStreetMap. Missing lanes and speed limits are filled with national defaults (Irkutsk 58% / 60%, Wrocław 45% / 21% of segments); building use is estimated from tags, land use and points of interest (4–8% of floor area stays unknown); building age is dropped where OSM has too few dates. Class names are written by hand from the cluster profiles shown in the legend.

Reading the map

NAIN
Normalised angular integration: how easy a street is to reach from everywhere within the radius.
NACH
Normalised angular choice: how often a street lies on the simplest routes, i.e. through-movement.
Rn, 800 m
Radius of the analysis: the whole network, or 800 m along the streets.
T0…T3, C0…C5
Transport and context classes, numbered by through-movement and by organisations per 100 m (low → high).
C_null
Segments with too little building data for a reliable context class.
Silhouette
How well a segment fits its class rather than the nearest other one (−1…1).
Borderline
A segment whose class is uncertain: low silhouette or low model confidence in either block.
ARI
Adjusted Rand index: agreement of two labellings, 1 = identical, 0 = chance.
