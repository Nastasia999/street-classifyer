About the method

Street Morphotypes: a data-driven street typology from OpenStreetMap

Anastasiia Erokhina · 2026

The question

A street is two things at once: a link in the movement network and a place lined by buildings and activities (Marshall 2005; Jones, Roberts & Morris 2007). Design guidance is increasingly written per street type, yet types are usually assigned by hand from the road hierarchy, which describes only the link. Can both roles be measured from open data alone, and does the resulting typology carry over from one city to another?

Method

  1. Segments. The OpenStreetMap network is split from junction to junction and where the street name changes; the two carriageways of a divided road keep their own sides.
  2. Link. Space syntax angular segment analysis: integration (NAIN) and through-movement (NACH) for the whole city (Rn) and within a 10-minute walk (800 m); lanes, public transport routes and stops.
  3. Place. Measured in one-sided strips beyond the carriageway edge: street-wall continuity, organisations, greenery and the floor-area shares of residential, commercial, social and industrial use.
  4. Two clusterings. Each block is clustered on its own (k-means, k chosen by silhouette), so neither swamps the other; where the silhouette cannot tell two values of k apart beyond its bootstrap noise, k is fixed by stability instead. The morphotype is the pair: transport class × context class. A Gaussian mixture gives each segment a confidence; uncertain segments are flagged as borderline.

Case study: Irkutsk and Wrocław

Two cities of similar size (620,000 and 670,000 people), each with a historic core and large post-war housing estates, processed with the same defaults: 4,528 and 7,294 classified segments.

  • The transport typology is universal. The four classes found independently in both cities (local access streets, ordinary streets, PT streets with stops, PT arterials) are practically the same: a city labelled by the other city's model agrees with its own labels at ARI 0.89–0.91.
  • The context typology has the same four types in both cities (ARI 0.67–0.73): residential, green, institutional and commercial streets. The thresholds are relative, though: by Wrocław's standards over a third of Irkutsk's commercial and institutional streets are ordinary residential streets. A typology meant to carry design guidance across cities should be trained on a diverse pool of cities, not on one.

Typologies

When a city was also labelled by another model, the Typology switch shows the same streets in up to three ways. Own: classes found in the city itself, what is typical there. Another city's model: the city measured by that city's classes, the direct test of whether a typology carries over. Pooled: one set of classes fitted on several cities together, the kind of typology design guidance needs, because a street type must mean the same in every city. Under Difference from own typology the map shows which streets change class, and the legend lists every change with its number of streets. In Irkutsk and Wrocław transport classes keep their streets under every model; context classes keep their types but move at the edges, and the pooled model, with only two context classes, merges the finer local ones.

Limitations

Everything rests on OpenStreetMap. Missing lanes and speed limits are filled with national defaults (Irkutsk 61% / 63%, Wrocław 47% / 22% of segments); building use is estimated from tags, land use and points of interest (4–8% of floor area stays unknown); building age is dropped where OSM has too few dates. Class names are generated from the cluster profiles shown in the legend, or written by hand from them.

Reading the map

NAIN
Normalised angular integration: how easy a street is to reach from everywhere within the radius.
NACH
Normalised angular choice: how often a street lies on the simplest routes, i.e. through-movement.
Rn, 800 m
Radius of the analysis: the whole network, or 800 m along the streets.
T1…Tk, C1…Cm
Transport and context classes, numbered by through-movement and by organisations per 100 m (low → high).
C_null
Segments with too little building data for a reliable context class.
Silhouette
How well a segment fits its class rather than the nearest other one (−1…1).
Borderline
A segment whose class is uncertain: low silhouette or low model confidence in either block.
ARI
Adjusted Rand index: agreement of two labellings, 1 = identical, 0 = chance.
